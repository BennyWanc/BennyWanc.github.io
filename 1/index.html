Images of the Russian Empire

CS280A Project 1 --- Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection

Overview

Each picture contains three grayscale exposures in B, G, R order. I held blue fixed, aligned green and red to blue, and stacked the aligned channels into an RGB image. The movement below use (x, y), which means x and y moves for x,y axis.

Single-scale alignment

For the small JPEGs, I searched translations in a 31×31 window centered at (0, 0). For every photo, I shifted the channel with np.roll, ignored the outer boundry problem, and used normalized cross-correlation (NCC). The candidate with the highest NCC was selected.

Multi-scale pyramid

Searching the shifting range on large TIFFs would be hard and time consuming. I resized each channel by half until its largest dimension is at most 400 pixels. I search ±15 pixels at the coarsest level, double that displacement at each finer level, and refine it within ±2 pixels. The TIFF results take about four seconds each on my machine.

Additional collection images

I selected three additional pictures from the Prokudin-Gorskii collection.

CS280A bells and whistles

Better features

There are some problem with original shifting on Emir. I compute gradient magnitude, √(dx²+dy²), and align edge images with NCC. This substantially improved Emir.

Automatic cropping

I first use the measured shifts to cut only the region that not shared by all three aligned channels. But the colored bpoarder re-exist. Then I choose to search outer 15% of the image, to mark dark pixels, bright pixels, I crop through the last row or column when more than 35% of it is marked.

There might be larger percent of colored pixels to cut. But I thought it will cut too much if enlarge the percentage. So this improved crop is kept.

Automatic contrast

I map the 0.5th intensity percentile to black and the 99.5th percentile to white, clipping values outside that range. Percentiles prevent a few scan-border pixels from controlling the entire contrast stretch.

Automatic white balance

I use the gray-world assumption: the average scene color estimates the illuminant. Each channel is scaled toward the shared mean, using half of the full correction to avoid aggressive casts. It helps some scenes, such as Icon, but can make Emir slightly greener because its scene average is not neutral. But overall, I think it is improvement.

Better color mapping

I apply a small 3×3 linear color transformation. Each output channel keeps most of its original value, receives a small boost, and subtracts a small contribution from the other two channels. The matrix is a simple visual correction rather than a recovery of the original filter responses.

Final Results

These images use the complete pipeline: gradient-feature pyramid alignment, automatic cropping, auto white balance, color mapping, and percentile contrast.
